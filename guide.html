Getting started

Waymark has three places, in the bar at the top: Maps (all your maps), the map you have open, and the Journal.

  1. On the Maps screen, tap + New map. Give it a name, pick Dungeon for anything with rooms or Region for overland travel, and type a grid size like 20x20.
  2. Pick a tool from the tool bar and draw with one finger. Use two fingers to move around and zoom.
  3. Made a mistake? Tap undo at the top, or press Ctrl Z on a keyboard.

On a phone, the most used tools sit in the bottom bar and the rest are under More. On a tablet you can move the tools to the left or right side in Settings.

Drawing a map

Rooms

Pick Room and drag a rectangle. Or type the size the GM gives you, like 6x4 or 30x20ft, and tap where it goes. Walls appear around it by themselves.

Paint

Paint floor squares freehand, which is handy for corridors. The tabs switch between indoor ground (stone, wood, water, ice, pits), outdoor terrain, and plain colours for marking areas. Start on a square that already has that ground to erase instead.

Walls and doors

Wall draws along grid lines; start on an existing wall to remove walls. Door places a door, locked door, secret door, bars, a low wall or ledge, or an opening. Tap the same door again to remove it.

Markers

Pick Marker and choose from four tabs: Basic shapes, Places (stairs, wells, graves, ruins), Finds (treasure, fuel, food, medicine) and Danger. Basic markers come in eight colours. Numbers and letters count up by themselves, arrows turn when you tap them again, and “your own symbol” can be any letter or emoji. Tap a marker again to mark it done (looted, disarmed, dealt with); a third tap removes it.

Pen, labels and notes

Pen draws freely, for caves, rivers and cliffs. Label puts a name on the map, such as “Crypt”. Note pins a numbered note to a square. Measure shows distances using Pathfinder’s rule for diagonals. Erase clears squares.

Random layouts

For practice fights or quick dungeons, open the ⋯ menu on the floor list and choose Random layout: rooms and corridors, caves, or a single room of the size you type, with doors, stairs, treasure, hazards and creatures if you want them.

Looking at a square

Pick Look and tap any square. A panel opens where you can write a note, add a label, change the marker or ground, and see where the square leads. The orange ring is the party: tap Move the party here to keep track of where you are.

Floors and exits between maps

The floor list sits in the corner of the map. Use + above and + below to add floors. Tap stairs with the Look tool and choose Add floor below to make the next floor with matching stairs.

  1. Pick Exit and tap the square that leads somewhere: cellar steps, a cave mouth, a road out of town.
  2. Choose where it goes: a new map, a map you already have, or another floor.
  3. Waymark opens that place. Tap the square where you arrive, and both ends are linked.

Each end gets a small tag such as “→ Cellar”. Tap Go there on a linked square, and use ← Back (or Android’s back button) to retrace your steps. The Maps screen shows how all your maps connect.

Travel routes

On a Region map, pick Route and tap squares in the order you travelled. Tap a stop again to mark a night’s camp. Set how many miles one square is and how far you travel in a day, and Waymark shows the distance and roughly how many days it took.

Journal and quick notes

The journal has sections for sessions, people, places, clues and rumours, open questions, goals and quests, expeditions, and lore. New notes start with a few useful prompts.

  • Press Q or tap Quick note to jot something down mid-game. It lands in the current session’s log with the time.
  • Link notes and maps by typing [[ and a name. Waymark suggests matches; press Tab to pick one.
  • Start a line with - [ ] for a checklist.
  • Notes on maps collects every note pinned to a square, so you can find them again.
  • Search everything with Ctrl K or the magnifying glass.

Your town’s supplies

The first journal section keeps a ledger of fuel, food, medicine, tools and materials. Tap + Brought home after an expedition, or − The town used when supplies run down. On a map, tap a supply marker with the Look tool and choose Log in the town ledger to record where it came from. You can rename the section to your own home base.

Characters from Pathbuilder

  1. In Pathbuilder 2e, open the menu and choose Export JSON. It shows a number.
  2. In Waymark, go to Journal, Characters, type the number and tap Import.

Waymark works out hit points, AC, saves, skills, Strikes and spells. After levelling up in Pathbuilder, export again and tap Update from Pathbuilder. Without internet, paste the exported text or pick the file instead.

Encounters

Setting up

Pick Tokens and tap a square to add one of your characters, a creature from your library, or a new creature with its HP, AC, saves and attacks. You can add several at once, like three wolves.

Running the fight

Tap the crossed swords to open the encounter. Start encounter rolls initiative and orders everyone; Next turn moves on and tracks rounds. Frightened goes down by itself at the end of a turn.

Moving

Drag a token. Waymark shades how far it can go with one, two or three Strides, going around walls, difficult ground and enemies, and counts the actions used.

Attacks and actions

Tap a token to see its card. Choose a target, then tap a Strike: Waymark rolls against the target’s AC with the multiple attack penalty, off-guard, flanking and frightened, rolls the damage and offers to apply it. The other actions, such as Trip, Grapple, Demoralize and Feint, roll the right skill against the right DC and add conditions like prone or frightened when they succeed.

Spells and areas

Spells come from the character’s list. Cast with one to three actions, roll a spell attack, have the target roll a save against your DC, or place the spell’s area. The Area tool draws bursts, emanations, cones and lines and lists who is inside. Waymark only knows spell names, so check each spell’s own rules.

Conditions and the creature library

Toggle conditions on the token card. Persistent damage is rolled at the end of each turn with the flat check to end it. Creatures you add are saved under Journal, Creatures, with space for what Recall Knowledge told you.

PDFs

Add character sheets and handouts under Journal, Documents, or share a PDF to Waymark from another app. They open in a built-in reader that works offline. PDFs aren’t part of backups, so keep the originals too.

Sharing maps with friends

  1. On the Maps screen, tap Send maps, or choose Send to the party from a map’s ⋯ menu.
  2. Choose one map, a map and everything it connects to, or all maps, then pick an app such as WhatsApp or Discord.
  3. Your friend opens the file with Waymark, or uses Receive maps.

Maps they already have are merged: their drawings stay, yours are added, and where you both wrote a note on the same square, both notes are kept. Tokens and encounters aren’t sent.

Backups

  1. Open Settings (☰) and tap Connect Google Drive.
  2. Tap Open google.com/device, sign in and type the code Waymark shows. If Google says it hasn’t verified the app, tap Advanced, then Go to Waymark.
  3. Come back to Waymark. It connects by itself and makes the first backup.

From then on, Waymark saves a dated backup to a “Waymark backups” folder in your Drive whenever you leave the app after changes, and keeps the newest 14 days. Without internet, it catches up next time. To get something back, use Restore in Settings: you see what’s in a backup first, and it’s merged with what you have.

Settings

Choose a dark or light look, put the tools on the left or right, show grid letters and numbers, show the floor below faintly, and turn on stylus mode, where the pen draws and fingers only move the map. Stylus mode switches on by itself the first time Waymark sees a stylus.
